Scan to align PNG borders

ONE SCAN PER STRIP

Print a numbered reference sheet, then scan its four cut strips separately. The reference marks measure where the printer puts the artwork relative to each paper edge.

  1. Prepare and print the reference sheet below. Keep the four numbered strips.
  2. Scan each strip at 300 or 600 DPI with a dark matte sheet behind it. Leave dark space around all four paper edges. Turn off automatic cropping, resizing and perspective correction.
  3. Upload strips 1–4, check the green outlines trace the actual cut paper, then calculate and review the correction.

Negative scanned margins mean an edge would be outside the paper. Margins are approximate millimeters at 300 DPI. Green outlines must follow the paper edge, not a printed border. Scanner detection and printer repeatability limit accuracy.

After applying, print and scan a fresh reference to verify the result. Ordinary photo-strip scans cannot be measured reliably here; use this page’s numbered reference sheet. Preparing, uploading and calculating never print or change alignment.

Calibration — strip centering

{% for i in range(1,5) %} {% endfor %}

Positive moves photos right; negative moves photos left. Calibration moves the photos within each strip. PNGs use their own controls below and remain fully visible. Accepted photo offsets: +20, +15, +6, −2 pixels. Custom offsets apply to future printed sheets and dry runs; they have not been physically qualified.

Calibration sheet & caliper measurements

Preview full-resolution calibration sheet

Print sends one sheet with the displayed target ID and its frozen settings. Never automatically retried. Check CUPS and paper after an uncertain submission before acknowledging.

On the identified printed sheet, keep strips in left-to-right order. At the middle gauge, measure from each cut edge to the center of its nearby vertical line, in millimeters. On strip 1, measure top/bottom cut edges to the center of the horizontal lines. Check the square is about 20 × 20 mm; this correction translates content, never rescales it.

New X = printed X + (right − left) × 300 / 25.4 / 2. Positive moves right. New Y = printed Y + (bottom − top) × 300 / 25.4 / 2. Positive moves down. Round the final value to the nearest pixel; exact halves round to even. Values outside ±60 pixels are rejected.
